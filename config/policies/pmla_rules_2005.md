# Prevention of Money Laundering (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005

Summary of key provisions from the PMLA Rules relevant to KYC verification and identity authentication for reporting entities in India.

## Rule 9: Verification of Identity and Address

Every reporting entity shall, at the time of commencement of an account-based relationship, verify the identity and address of the client using an Officially Valid Document (OVD) as defined under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act.

For **individuals**, the reporting entity must obtain:
- One certified copy of an OVD containing the name, address, and photograph of the client.
- The Permanent Account Number (PAN) or equivalent e-document thereof, or Form 60 where PAN has not been allotted.
- For transactions above Rs. 50,000 (whether in cash or otherwise), PAN is mandatory.

For **non-individuals** (companies, trusts, partnerships), the reporting entity must additionally obtain:
- Registration certificate, partnership deed, trust deed, or certificate of incorporation as applicable.
- PAN of the entity and of the beneficial owners holding more than 25% ownership or control.

Verification must be completed before the account is made operational. In cases where verification is permitted to be deferred (low-risk scenarios under Simplified Due Diligence), the deferral period shall not exceed six months from account opening.

The reporting entity shall maintain records of all documents obtained for verification for a period of five years after the business relationship has ended or after the date of the transaction, whichever is later.

## Rule 9A: e-KYC Authentication

Aadhaar-based electronic Know Your Customer (e-KYC) authentication is permitted as a valid method of Customer Due Diligence, subject to the following conditions:

1. **Consent**: The client must provide informed, voluntary consent for authentication of their Aadhaar number. The consent must be recorded and maintained by the reporting entity.

2. **Authentication modes**: Both OTP-based and biometric-based Aadhaar authentication are accepted. OTP-based authentication may result in accounts with limited operational features unless upgraded through biometric or in-person verification within a prescribed period.

3. **Data handling**: The demographic information and photograph received from UIDAI through e-KYC shall constitute valid proof of identity and address. The reporting entity shall not store the Aadhaar number or biometric data beyond the purpose of authentication.

4. **Accounts opened via OTP-based e-KYC** are classified as accounts with reduced CDD. Such accounts shall have the following restrictions until upgraded:
   - Aggregate balance shall not exceed Rs. 1,00,000.
   - Aggregate of all credits in a financial year shall not exceed Rs. 2,00,000.
   - Aggregate of all withdrawals and transfers in a month shall not exceed Rs. 10,000.

5. **Upgrade**: An account opened through OTP-based e-KYC may be converted to a fully KYC-compliant account upon completion of biometric authentication or through in-person verification (IPV) by an authorized official of the reporting entity, or via Video-based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP).

Reporting entities must ensure that their e-KYC processes comply with the Aadhaar (Authentication) Regulations issued by UIDAI and with the directions of their sectoral regulator.
