# RBI KYC Master Direction, 2016 (Updated)

Summary of key provisions from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Master Direction on Know Your Customer (KYC) norms, issued under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002.

## Section 3: Definitions

**Customer Due Diligence (CDD)** means identifying the customer and verifying their identity using reliable, independent source documents, data, or information. CDD includes ongoing monitoring of transactions to ensure consistency with the institution's knowledge of the customer.

**Know Your Customer (KYC)** refers to the process of verifying the identity and address of a customer at the time of account opening and periodically thereafter. KYC norms are mandatory for all Regulated Entities (REs) including banks, NBFCs, and payment system operators.

**Officially Valid Document (OVD)** means any of the following documents: (a) Passport, (b) Driving Licence, (c) Proof of possession of Aadhaar number, (d) Voter's Identity Card issued by the Election Commission of India, (e) Job card issued by NREGA duly signed by a State Government officer, (f) Letter issued by the National Population Register containing details of name and address.

**Permanent Account Number (PAN)** issued by the Income Tax Department is mandatory for transactions above Rs. 50,000 and for opening accounts with banks and financial institutions, as per Section 139A of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

## Section 38: Customer Identification — OVD Requirements

Every Regulated Entity shall verify the identity and address of a customer using Officially Valid Documents before establishing an account-based relationship. The following documents are accepted as OVDs for identity verification:

1. **PAN Card**: Mandatory for all accounts. If PAN is not available, Form 60 must be obtained. PAN serves as the primary proof of identity and tax compliance. Format: five letters, four digits, one letter (e.g., ABCDE1234F).

2. **Aadhaar (Proof of possession of Aadhaar number)**: Accepted as OVD for both identity and address verification. Aadhaar-based e-KYC is permitted for Customer Due Diligence, subject to customer consent. The 12-digit Aadhaar number along with biometric or OTP authentication constitutes valid identification.

3. **Passport**: Valid for identity and address proof. Current passports with legible photographs are accepted.

4. **Driving Licence**: Valid for identity and address proof when current and issued by the relevant transport authority.

5. **Voter's Identity Card**: Issued by the Election Commission, accepted for identity verification.

For accounts opened using Aadhaar-based e-KYC, the process must comply with Section 11A of the PMLA and the Aadhaar Act, 2016. Customer consent must be obtained before authentication.

## Section 52: Simplified Due Diligence (SDD)

Simplified Due Diligence measures may be applied where the Regulated Entity assesses low risk of money laundering or terrorist financing. SDD is applicable to:

- Small accounts with balances not exceeding Rs. 50,000 and total credits not exceeding Rs. 1,00,000 in a year.
- Accounts opened with self-certification of OVDs for low-value transactions.
- Walk-in customers conducting occasional transactions below the prescribed threshold.

Under SDD, the Regulated Entity may accept a self-certified copy of an OVD, defer verification for a period not exceeding six months, and apply reduced ongoing monitoring. However, if suspicious activity is detected, full CDD must be applied immediately.

## Section 56: Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

Enhanced Due Diligence must be applied in situations presenting a higher risk of money laundering. EDD measures include:

- Obtaining additional identity documents beyond the minimum OVD requirement.
- Conducting more frequent reviews and monitoring of transactions.
- Verifying source of funds and purpose of the account relationship.
- Senior management approval for establishing or continuing the business relationship.

EDD is mandatory for: (a) Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) and their relatives, (b) Non-face-to-face customers, (c) Customers from high-risk jurisdictions identified by FATF, (d) Accounts showing unusual or suspicious transaction patterns.

The Regulated Entity must document the rationale for applying EDD and maintain records for a minimum of five years after the business relationship has ended.
